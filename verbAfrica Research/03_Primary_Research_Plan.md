# verbAfrica — Primary Research Plan
*Last updated: March 2026 | To be executed before business plan is finalised*

---

## Why This Matters

All the desk research in the world won't tell you why a specific Jhb photographer charges R3,500 for a shoot but gets ghosted on payment, or why a Sandton marketing manager ended up booking a DJ through a friend instead of searching online. That texture only comes from real conversations.

The goal of primary research is not to validate your assumptions. It's to break them.

Target: **10 creative conversations + 5 client conversations** before writing the business plan.
Timeline: 2–3 weeks.

---

## Part 1 — Research With Creatives

### Who to Talk To

You want working creatives in Jhb — people who are actively taking bookings, not hobbyists. Target:

- 2–3 DJs (one club, one events/corporate, one up-and-coming)
- 2–3 photographers/videographers (one events, one content/commercial)
- 1–2 music artists (one established, one emerging)
- 1–2 content creators / social media focused
- 1 MC or event host

Mix of experience levels. You want someone who's been doing this 5+ years AND someone who started 1–2 years ago. The pain points differ.

### Where to Find Them

**Instagram:** Search these hashtags and DM profiles that look active and working (recent posts, decent following, clear professional work):
- #joburgphotographer
- #joburgsound
- #jhbmusic
- #johannesburgdj
- #joburgevents
- #jhbcontentcreator
- #joburgsessions

**Facebook Groups to join:**
- "Johannesburg Creatives"
- "SA Freelancers"
- "Joburg Events Network"
- Search "SA music industry" + "SA photography"

**Your existing network:** If you or your team know any creatives personally, start there. A warm introduction is worth ten cold DMs.

---

### The DM Script (Instagram / WhatsApp)

Keep it short. Don't pitch verbAfrica. Ask for their time and be honest about why.

> Hey [Name], I came across your work — really rate it. I'm building something for SA creatives and I'm trying to understand how the industry actually works before I build anything. Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation? Just your experience finding clients, getting paid, that kind of thing. Happy to do it over call or WhatsApp voice — whatever works for you. No pitch, just listening.

**What makes this work:**
- Compliments their work specifically (don't copy-paste without looking)
- Is honest about what you're building (don't hide it)
- Frames it as you listening, not them evaluating
- Offers flexibility on format
- Is short — no one reads long DMs from strangers

Expect a 20–30% response rate. Send to 40–50 people to get your 10 conversations.

---

### Creative Interview Guide

**Before the conversation:** Do 2 minutes of research on them. Know their category, roughly how established they look, and one specific thing about their work to reference.

**Opening (2 min):**
> Thanks for making time. Like I said, I'm trying to understand the real picture of how creatives in Jhb find and manage their work. I'm going to ask you about your actual experience — no right or wrong answers, I just want to hear how it really works for you.

**The Questions (15–20 min):**

*On finding work:*
1. Think about your last 5 bookings. Where did each of them come from? (Let them count on their fingers — be specific, not general)
2. What percentage of your work comes from returning clients vs. new clients?
3. When a new client finds you for the first time, what usually triggers it? (Referral? Instagram? They searched something?)
4. Have you ever tried any platform or app to find work — Bark, Bookings Africa, anything? What happened?

*On the booking process:*
5. Walk me through what happens from the moment a new client first contacts you to the moment you're confirmed for the job. Be specific.
6. What do you send a client when they ask about your rates and services? (Do they have a rate card? A PDF? Do they quote verbally?)
7. How often does a potential booking fall through, and why?

*On payment:*
8. How does payment usually work for you? (terms, method, timing)
9. Have you ever not been paid, or been paid late? What happened?
10. If you could change one thing about how payment works in your industry, what would it be?

*On professional presentation:*
11. Do you have an EPK? (If yes: how do you use it? Who asks for it? If no: have clients ever asked for one?)
12. When a client is deciding between you and someone else, what do you think usually makes them pick you?

*On the platform idea (save for the end):*
13. If there was a platform where clients in Jhb could search for creatives like you, see your full profile, rate card, and EPK, and book you — would you join? What would make you trust it enough to actually use it?
14. What would make you NOT join?

**Closing:**
> This has been really useful — exactly what I needed. Can I reach back out to you in a few months when we're closer to launching something? And do you know anyone else I should talk to?

---

### What to Listen For

**Red flags that should change your product:**
- "I already have more work than I can handle" — if most say this, discovery isn't the problem
- "Clients don't trust platforms" — you need a trust-building strategy, not just a good product
- "I'd never pay a commission" — you need to hear the actual number where they'd say no

**Green lights that validate the thesis:**
- "Finding new clients is my biggest challenge" — discovery problem confirmed
- "I've been ghosted on payment multiple times" — escrow becomes non-negotiable feature
- "I don't have a proper EPK, clients ask for it sometimes" — EPK angle validated
- "I tried Bookings Africa but..." — competitive intelligence and what to avoid

## Part 2 — Research With Clients

### Who to Talk To

Small-to-medium business owners in Jhb who regularly need creative services. Target:

- 1–2 restaurant / hospitality owners (DJ for events, photographers for content)
- 1–2 boutique brand owners or marketing managers (content creators, photographers)
- 1 event planner or promoter (full stack — DJs, MCs, photographers)
- 1 agency creative director (understands both sides)

### Where to Find Them

- LinkedIn: search "marketing manager Johannesburg SME" or browse companies in Sandton/Rosebank/Fourways
- Instagram: small Jhb businesses with active content often hire creatives — DM the brand account and ask to speak to whoever manages their content
- Your network: does anyone on your team know a restaurant owner, a brand person, an events company?

---

### Client Interview Guide

**Opening:**
> I'm researching how businesses in Jhb find and book creative talent — photographers, DJs, content creators, that kind of thing. I want to understand how it actually works from the client side. Can I ask you about a recent time you needed to hire someone?

**The Questions:**

*On finding creatives:*
1. Think about the last time you hired a photographer, DJ, or content creator. How did you find them?
2. Did you look at more than one person? How did you compare them?
3. How long did the whole process take, from "I need someone" to "confirmed"?
4. Have you ever used a platform or app to find creative talent? What was your experience?

*On the decision process:*
5. What made you trust the person you ended up booking?
6. What information do you wish you'd had upfront that you had to dig for?
7. Have you ever had a bad experience booking a creative? What happened?

*On payment and trust:*
8. How did payment work? Were you comfortable with it?
9. Would you use a platform that held payment in escrow — paid upfront but only released to the creative when the job was done? Would that make you more likely to book someone new?

*On the platform idea:*
10. If there was a directory of vetted Jhb creatives with standardised profiles — photo, video samples, rate card, reviews — would you use it? What would you need to see to trust it?
11. What would put you off using something like that?

## Part 3 — Observational Research

### Join and Observe (Don't Post Yet)

Join these communities and spend two weeks reading before you write anything:

- Johannesburg-based creative Facebook groups
- Any SA creative WhatsApp groups you can get access to
- Instagram comments on active SA creative accounts

**What to note:**
- How do people talk about finding work?
- What complaints come up repeatedly?
- What language do creatives use to describe themselves? (This is your copy)
- When someone posts looking for a photographer or DJ, how do responses work?

### Test Bookings Africa From Both Sides

1. Register as a creative on Bookings Africa. Fill out your profile. Note what the experience is like, what's unclear, what's friction-heavy.
2. Go back as a client and try to find and book someone for a fictional event. Note where the experience breaks.

This is 2 hours of your time and will tell you more about the competitive landscape than any amount of desk research.

### Post a Fake Brief

Post this (or similar) in a SA creative Facebook group or WhatsApp group:

> Looking for a photographer in Sandton for a product shoot. Budget R3,000–4,000, half-day shoot, ASAP. Prefer someone with commercial/brand experience. Please DM me with your rates and portfolio.

Observe: How many responses? How fast? What do they send? What's the quality like? How many have a proper rate card vs. a voice note vs. nothing? This gives you the baseline you're replacing.

## Capturing Your Research

After each conversation, write down:

1. The person's category, years working, rough income level if they shared
2. The three most important things they said (verbatim where possible)
3. What surprised you
4. What confirmed your assumptions
5. Any names or platforms they mentioned

Bring the notes back to me. I'll help you synthesise them into themes, identify patterns, and update the business plan assumptions accordingly.
