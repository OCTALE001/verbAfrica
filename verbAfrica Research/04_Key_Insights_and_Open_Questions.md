## What We Know (Desk Research)

### About the Market

- SA's creative economy contributes R90B+ annually but punches below its weight digitally (ranked 59th globally in online freelance)
- The informal creative market in Jhb runs entirely on Instagram + WhatsApp + word of mouth — no central directory exists
- 19.29% of SA gig workers are in creative/multimedia — the talent pool is significant
- African freelance market growing at 18.1% CAGR through 2030
- Non-payment is a documented, persistent pain point for SA creative freelancers

### About the Competition

- **Bookings Africa** is the most direct precedent. 8,000 registered creatives, escrow payments, 4-stage vetting — but stalled on beta since at least 2024. Nigeria-centric. Under-capitalised ($20K raised).
- **No competitor currently occupies SA-first + entertainment-specific.** That position is unoccupied.
- **Bark.com** already operates in SA (R100/lead) — proof that SA creatives will pay for discovery when it works.
- **GigSalad** is the north star at scale: free-to-list → paid visibility → 2.5% commission. $113M paid to talent.
- The real competition is the DM on Instagram, not any formal platform.

### About the Product

- verbAfrica's prototype already has features no competitor has: EPK builder, brief posting, shortlist + compare, creative portal with dashboard
- The EPK is the strongest differentiator in the market — no other platform standardises this
- Payment and vetting are the two biggest missing features for a functional marketplace

### About the Business

- Solo founder (Alexander October) building with Claude as technical partner
- 10-creative curated cohort — invitation only, not open registration
- First client target: Johannesburg small businesses, entrepreneurs, event organisers
- Revenue model: not yet defined (concierge MVP first — no backend)
- Success at December 2026: every cohort member can say *"I'm getting bookings through verbAfrica, verbAfrica has helped me build a strong business foundation, and levelled up my game through tech"* — see `Strategy/01_Vision.md §4`

## Hypotheses to Test

These are specific beliefs that the primary research should either confirm or break.

**H1: The biggest pain for Jhb creatives is finding new clients, not managing existing ones.**
*Implication if true:* Discovery and profile visibility are the core product priorities.
*Implication if false:* The product should focus on operational tools (invoicing, scheduling, brief management) more than discovery.

**H2: Non-payment is a big enough problem that escrow would be a purchase driver for clients.**
*Implication if true:* Build escrow before monetisation — it's the trust layer.
*Implication if false:* Escrow can be deprioritised; focus on discovery first.

**H3: Jhb creatives are willing to pay a 5–10% commission on bookings made through the platform.**
*Implication if true:* GigSalad-style transaction fee model is viable.
*Implication if false:* Needs to be a subscription or lead-based model instead.

**H4: The EPK is something most creatives don't have but clients frequently ask for.**
*Implication if true:* EPK builder is the #1 supply-side acquisition tool.
*Implication if false:* EPK is a nice-to-have, not the lead feature.

**H5: Small Jhb businesses prefer to hire from personal networks because platforms feel risky.**
*Implication if true:* Trust-building (vetting, reviews, escrow) must come before discovery.
*Implication if false:* A good discovery product is sufficient to attract clients.

## Strategic Tensions (Things That Pull in Opposite Directions)

Understanding these tensions is important before writing the business plan.

**Speed vs. quality**
You want to launch in 1–3 months. But a marketplace without enough supply density doesn't work. Rushing to launch before 50+ quality creatives are profiled could result in a first client experience that kills word of mouth.

**Supply-first vs. demand-first**
Every marketplace must choose which side to fix first. The evidence suggests supply is harder in entertainment marketplaces (creatives are skeptical). But verbAfrica's bootstrapped status means you can't sustain a supply-only phase indefinitely.

**Free vs. paid**
Free-to-list is the fastest way to get supply. But if you're bootstrapped, you need revenue. The tension: how long can you be free before you need to charge? GigSalad did it for years. verbAfrica doesn't have that runway.

**Jhb-first vs. national**
The business case is strongest if you own Jhb deeply before going national. But if a competitor launches in Cape Town while you're focused on Jhb, you lose that market by default.

**EPK as differentiator vs. EPK as barrier**
The EPK is a powerful differentiator. It's also additional friction in onboarding. Creatives who don't have an EPK might feel intimidated. The product needs to make EPK creation feel like a gift, not a requirement.

## Current Hypothesis on Differentiator

Based on research so far, verbAfrica's strongest differentiator is a combination of three things:

1. **SA-first positioning** — built for Jhb, in SA cultural context, not Nigeria repurposed
2. **The EPK layer** — standardised professional presentation that no competitor has
3. **Premium UX and brand** — looks and feels like something a Sandton brand manager would trust, not a scrappy startup

The positioning that feels truest: **"verbAfrica is where African creative professionals present themselves seriously and where brands find them."**

The word "seriously" does a lot of work there. It implies the platform itself takes creatives seriously. That the presentation is professional. That clients can trust what they find.

*This is a hypothesis. Primary research may change it.*

## What Needs to Be True for verbAfrica to Win

Working backwards from the 6-month pilot goal (50+ creatives, first booking, first payment):

1. At least 50 creatives have complete, quality profiles (not just registered — actually profiled)
2. At least 10 Jhb SMB clients have visited the platform and used it to search
3. At least 1 booking has happened through the platform (not offline)
4. At least 1 person has paid for something (listing upgrade, lead, or commission)
5. The product works end-to-end: search → profile → brief → booking → payment → review

Most marketplace failures happen because founders celebrate supply numbers (registered creatives) without checking if the marketplace is actually working (bookings happening). Keep the focus on whether money moves.
